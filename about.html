About

Emir Brown-Murillo

Brown co-founded and led Free Thought Poetry at Emory University and previously headed the poetry committee for INK, the literary magazine at Democracy Prep Charter High School. They now serve as a poetry reader for Broad Ripple Review. They developed Ouroboron couplets, a poetic form consisting of four to twelve couplets that represent a self-consuming cycle.

A poet from Harlem River Houses in New York City, Brown writes about home, race, gender, language, and institutional power. Their work moves between sonnets, couplets, lyric poems, and prose, drawing on the different registers of family, neighborhood, and academic life.

Brown earned a B.A. in Comparative Literature from Emory University in 2021. Their literary development has included an artist residency at the Anderson Center in 2020 and recognition as a finalist for the Brooklyn Poets Winter Fellowship and the Charles S. Longcope Jr. Writers & Artist Grant in 2026. At Emory, they also received the 2020 J. Michael Aycock Leadership Award.

Alongside their writing, Brown works in education in New York City. They taught fifth- and sixth-grade English at Uncommon Canarsie Middle School and have served as an education specialist and interim program director at Roads to Success. Their work includes developing literacy curriculum, supporting instructors, and guiding youth programs across multiple sites. They have also volunteered as a youth soccer coach with South Bronx United.

Brown is the author of three poetry books and the unpublished collection CONSTITUENT. A 2020 Frost Place Latinx Poetry Scholar, they received Emory University’s Artistine Mann Award in Poetry in 2017 and Excellence in LGBTQ+ Writing Award in 2020. Their books and selected journal and anthology publications are gathered on the Selected Works page.

They also created Poetry Processor, a digital writing tool for poets.
